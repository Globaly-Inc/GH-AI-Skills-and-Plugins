# Forcing Questions

Ask **one at a time**, each with your own recommended answer. The point is discomfort: "Specificity is the
only currency." Prefer evidence (real behavior) over opinion (interest, signups, enthusiasm). If the
codebase or web research can answer a question, do that instead of asking.

## Startup lens (relentless — when validating a real bet)

1. **Demand reality** — What concrete behavior proves someone needs this? Not interest, not a signup — what
   did someone actually *do* that shows the pain is real?
2. **Status quo** — What workaround are users living with today (a competitor, a spreadsheet, duct tape,
   nothing)? What does that workaround cost them? The status quo is the real competitor.
3. **Desperate specificity** — Name **one actual person**: their role, their situation, and the concrete
   consequence they face without this. "Users" and "teams" are not answers.
4. **Narrowest wedge** — What is shippable this week that one person would pay real money for? Strip
   everything else.
5. **Observation & surprise** — What did you learn watching someone use it (or the status quo) *without your
   help*? What surprised you?
6. **Future-fit** — Why does this become **more** essential as the world changes (AI, regulation, market),
   not less? Ground this with web/market research, not a guess.

## Builder lens (collaborative — when exploring for delight)

1. **Delight** — What would make this genuinely delightful to use, not merely functional?
2. **Most exciting version** — If effort were free, what's the version you'd be *thrilled* to build?
3. **Fastest path to "worth showing"** — What's the smallest thing you could put in front of people this
   week that would make them lean in?
4. **Pride** — What would you be proud to demo? What would embarrass you to ship?
5. **Energy** — Which part of this are you most excited to work on? (Momentum matters in Builder mode.)

## Honesty rules

- Take a position on every answer. If you think the demand evidence is weak, say so and say what would
  change your mind.
- Disconfirming evidence is gold — actively look for the fact that would prove the idea wrong.
- Never launder a single anecdote into a "trend."
